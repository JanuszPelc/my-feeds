<h2>European Commission Proposes Digital Fairness Act with New Restrictions on Video Games</h2>
<a name="story-27437"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fmedia.licdn.com%2Fdms%2Fimage%2Fv2%2FD4D22AQFbfpMcSa_R6w%2Ffeedshare-shrink_1280%2FB4DZnFuA90JcAw-%2F0%2F1759958798765%3Fe%3D2147483647%26v%3Dbeta%26t%3D0Cgh2FfhdBORCRKaxkAJ96qDyn29ydijqTR_swQAdSY&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>The European Commission has released its proposal for the Digital Fairness Act, introducing strict consumer protection rules that target monetization and user experience design in video games across European Union member states. The 71-page legislative proposal includes sweeping restrictions on free-to-play features, requiring transparent real-money values for virtual currencies, prohibiting certain pay-to-win mechanisms for minors, and banning the sale of loot boxes to underage players entirely.</p><p>Additional provisions require companies to allow users to disable compulsive design elements such as push notifications and engagement streaks, while virtual currencies and in-app purchases targeting minors will be disabled by default. European industry trade bodies, including Video Games Europe and the European Games Developer Federation, have criticized the proposal, arguing that its broad definitions and age-rating criteria could heavily disrupt game development and balancing before the bill faces a formal vote in the European Parliament and Council.</p>
