<h2>iFixit iPhone Duo Teardown Explores Hinge Design and Early Screen Failures</h2>
<p>iFixit has published a comprehensive teardown of Apple's first foldable smartphone, the iPhone Duo, examining its complex hinge mechanism, nano-texture display finish, and custom subpixel layouts. While the device incorporates accessible repair features like electricity-releasing batteries and a modular outer cover layer, out-of-warranty screen replacements remain exceptionally expensive and difficult.</p><p>The teardown also investigated early reports of screen damage and bright lines along the fold, utilizing 3D modeling and CT scans to analyze internal stresses. The investigation suggests that early screen failures may cluster near a mechanical hotspot where internal support components interact with the folding display during closure.</p>

<h2>Dolly Parton&#x27;s Imagination Library Provides Free Books to Children Worldwide</h2>
<p>Dolly Parton’s Imagination Library is an international book gifting program that mails free, age-appropriate books every month to children from birth to age five. The initiative aims to foster a love of reading early in life and ensure that children have access to books regardless of their family's income.</p><p>Founded by Parton in 1995 in her home county of East Tennessee, the program has since expanded internationally to operate across the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, Australia, and the Republic of Ireland. It is sustained through a shared funding model between Parton's foundation and local community partners.</p>

<h2>SimCity&#x27;s Performance Optimization Techniques for 16 MHz Hardware Examined</h2>
<p>The article examines how early development teams successfully ran complex simulation games like the original 1989 SimCity on constrained 16 MHz hardware. By analyzing the open-source Micropolis codebase, the piece highlights how core simulation systems—such as traffic, fire, and crime—were updated across multiple frames rather than all at once every frame.</p><p>This technique of distributing computational workloads across frames avoids performance bottlenecks without noticeably degrading gameplay responsiveness. The author notes that similar frame-budgeting principles extend to modern graphics and rendering pipelines, such as reflection cubemaps in Assetto Corsa and dynamic lighting in Unreal Engine.</p>
<p><small>Links: <a href="https://larstofus.com/2025/11/16/how-to-run-a-city-builder-on-12-5-mhz/">larstofus.com</a> | <a href="https://lobste.rs/s/qzfohf/how_run_city_builder_on_16_mhz">discussion</a> | <a href="https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50194536">hacker news</a></small></p>

<h2>Project Hedra Open-Source 3D RPG and Custom Game Engine</h2>
<p>Project Hedra is an open-world, low-poly 3D role-playing game paired with a custom game engine built completely from scratch. It features procedurally generated infinite worlds, quest systems, character classes, skill trees, and a modern OpenGL rendering pipeline.</p><p>Developed initially by maxilevi while in high school to learn computer graphics, the codebase spans approximately 100,000 lines of C#. The custom engine incorporates advanced technical features including runtime shader reloading, IronPython-based modding support, custom memory allocators to minimize garbage collection, and an entity component system.</p>

<h2>Isaac Marovitz Examines Unused Visual Features and Rendering Systems in Sonic 06</h2>
<p>Developer Isaac Marovitz explores the technical reasons behind the downgraded visuals of Sonic the Hedgehog (2006), detailing how performance constraints during crunch led Sonic Team to disable advanced shaders, shadow map cascades, and point lights. Through work on the Marathon Recompiled project, the analysis reveals that many higher-fidelity visual assets and effects from beta builds remain dormant in the game files.</p><p>The article examines specific rendering limitations, such as the widespread reliance on rudimentary vertex-lit shaders like Billboard02, unoptimized point light counts that overload specific stages, and disabled real-time planar reflections in multiplayer modes. While some of these systems can be tweaked or stabilized via recompilation and patching, swapping core shader variants remains challenging due to complex material definitions and input layouts.</p>
<p><small>Links: <a href="https://isaacmarovitz.com/posts/sonic-06/">isaacmarovitz.com</a> | <a href="https://lobste.rs/s/nb7wkp/many_many_missed_opportunities_sonic_06">discussion</a></small></p>

<h2>Oura Sues Ultrahuman Again to Block U.S. Sales of Ring Pro</h2>
<p>Smart ring maker Oura has filed a patent infringement lawsuit against rival Ultrahuman in a federal court in Texas, seeking monetary damages and a permanent injunction to block U.S. sales of the Ring Pro. The complaint alleges that the redesigned smart ring infringes two of Oura's patents covering device design and internal components, including a patent issued just days before the filing.</p><p>The legal action threatens Ultrahuman's return to the American market, which it achieved earlier this year following a previous import ban stemming from an International Trade Commission ruling against its earlier Ring Air model. Oura has pursued similar patent claims against other smart ring competitors, including Samsung and RingConn, as litigation intensifies across the growing wearable health market.</p>

<h2>Why Prioritizing Core Teams in Game Development Risks Devaluing Other Developers</h2>
<p>An opinion article examines how media coverage of distributed game development often attributes large-scale projects entirely to small core teams, inadvertently obscuring the contributions of hundreds of external and support developers. The piece analyzes recent discussions surrounding games like Star Wars: Galactic Racer and Clair Obscur: Expedition 33, where lean core staffing figures were highlighted while thousands of credited contributors went unmentioned.</p><p>The author argues that framing game success around an exclusive core group reinforces problematic industry hierarchies, impacts career visibility, and influences how investors and studios evaluate talent during funding and layoffs. By examining the linguistic choices in game journalism and marketing, the article calls for more precise terminology that acknowledges distributed development models without erasing the broader workforce.</p>
