<h2>Marissa Mayer Criticized for Comments on Gender and AI in New York Times Interview</h2>
<a name="story-25564"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fstorage.ghost.io%2Fc%2F0f%2F76%2F0f76b548-bc58-4f25-abc3-3f5ebca07da4%2Fcontent%2Fimages%2F2026%2F10%2FSan_Francisco_Chronicle_Archive_Image_23130584607860.jpg&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>Former Yahoo CEO Marissa Mayer faced widespread public criticism following a New York Times profile in which she promoted her new AI assistant, Dazzle, and dismissed the ongoing relevance of gender bias in the technology industry. During the interview, Mayer revealed she used her AI tool to generate descriptive qualities for her young daughter, while asserting that passion acts as a gender-neutralizing force that erases systemic barriers.</p><p>Observers and industry reports quickly contrasted Mayer's remarks with stark professional disparities, including lower funding levels for female-founded startups, high attrition rates among women in tech, and recent high-profile setbacks such as the cancellation of the Grace Hopper Celebration. The backlash also follows broader federal rollbacks of diversity programs and research grants that have disproportionately affected women and early-career researchers in STEM fields.</p>

<h2>General Compute Raises $100 Million Series A at $800 Million Valuation</h2>
<a name="story-25560"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Ftheaiinsider.tech%2Fwp-content%2Fuploads%2F2026%2F01%2FScreenshot-2026-01-27-at-06.28.10-768x856.png&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>General Compute has raised $100 million in a Series A funding round led by Blackbird Ventures and Abstract Ventures, bringing its post-money valuation to $800 million. <h2>Venkatesh Rao Publishes Essay on Cognitive Mercantilism and AI Panic</h2>
<a name="story-25441"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fsubstackcdn.com%2Fimage%2Ffetch%2F%24s_%210-U5%21%2Cw_1456%2Cc_limit%2Cf_auto%2Cq_auto%3Agood%2Cfl_progressive%3Asteep%2Fhttps%253A%252F%252Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%252Fpublic%252Fimages%252Fcafd3438-ef0d-41c0-83cf-bcc7e1c995be_1536x1024.png&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>In his essay "Cognitive Mercantilism," Venkatesh Rao argues that current societal anxieties around artificial intelligence structurally resemble the mercantilist worldview that preceded modern capitalism. He contends that contemporary critics treat intelligence as a scarce, fixed resource to be hoarded by elites, much like early modern economists viewed land and bullion.</p><p>Drawing parallels to Joyce Appleby’s history of capitalism, Rao suggests that abundant AI will shift the economic paradigm from cognitive production to consumption. Just as historical agricultural advances allowed populations to become consumers rather than solely manual laborers, AI-driven abundance could spark a "cognitive industrious revolution" where ordinary individuals leverage vast intelligence for new ambitions and projects.</p>
