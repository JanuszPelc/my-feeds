<h2>DoorDash Agrees to Acquire Instacart for $17 Billion</h2>
<a name="story-25849"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Famppfy.com%2Fwp-content%2Fuploads%2F2025%2F08%2F20250827_1334_Boost-Earnings-with-Delivery_simple_compose_01k3pkqypge0m9d10gz7pn6xy0-1200x800.png&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>DoorDash has entered into a definitive agreement to acquire Instacart for approximately $17 billion, valuing the grocery technology company at $65.00 per share. The transaction combines DoorDash's restaurant and local delivery network with Instacart's grocery fulfillment technology, uniting two major delivery platforms to expand merchant tools and consumer choices.</p><p>Under the terms of the agreement, Instacart stockholders can elect to receive cash, DoorDash stock, or a combination of both. The deal is expected to close in late 2027 or the first half of 2028, pending approval by Instacart stockholders and regulatory agencies including antitrust clearances in the United States, Canada, and Israel. Until closing, both companies will continue to operate independently.</p>

<h2>US Battery Storage Installations Reach Record 20.2 GWh in Q2 2026</h2>
<a name="story-25844"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Felectrek.co%2Fwp-content%2Fuploads%2Fsites%2F3%2F2026%2F06%2FGreen-River-Energy-Center-Utah.jpg%3Fquality%3D82%26strip%3Dall%26w%3D1600&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>The United States added a record 20.2 gigawatt-hours of battery storage capacity in the second quarter of 2026, bringing total installations for the first half of the year to 30.8 GWh according to a report from the Solar Energy Industries Association and Benchmark Mineral Intelligence. Growth was overwhelmingly driven by utility-scale projects, which accounted for approximately 18 GWh of the quarterly total, led by heavy deployment in Arizona, Texas, and Utah.</p><p>While utility-scale and commercial segments expanded rapidly to meet rising grid demand and data center constraints, residential battery storage experienced a 27% year-over-year decline in Q2 following the removal of the federal 25D residential clean energy tax credit. Driven by stronger-than-expected demand, Benchmark raised its cumulative storage forecast through 2030 by 11.5% to 683 GWh, with projected total installations for 2026 reaching 71 GWh.</p>

<h2>Samsung Expects 2026 Capital Expenditures to Rise 60% Amid Strong AI Demand</h2>
<a name="story-25775"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.sammobile.com%2Fwp-content%2Fuploads%2F2024%2F02%2FSamsung-HBM3E-12H-DRAM-Chip.jpg&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>Samsung Electronics plans to increase its capital expenditures by roughly 60% this year to approximately $63 billion, driven largely by massive investments in chip manufacturing infrastructure. The company reported that its high-bandwidth memory capacity is completely sold out through 2027, backed by 10 long-term supply agreements secured with major customers.</p><p>The surge in spending comes as Samsung navigates a recovering semiconductor market propelled by artificial intelligence demand, even as its recent chip profit missed broader analyst estimates. The vast majority of the capital expenditure is earmarked for its Device Solutions division to expand advanced memory production capacity and secure long-term competitiveness.</p>
