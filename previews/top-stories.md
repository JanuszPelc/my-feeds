<h2>ICE and HSI Conduct Sweeping Surveillance Operation Targeting Left-Wing Groups</h2>
<a name="story-25693"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fa57.foxnews.com%2Fstatic.foxnews.com%2Ffoxnews.com%2Fcontent%2Fuploads%2F2026%2F02%2F1200%2F675%2Fmn-ice-plates-database-homeland-security-investigations.jpg%3Fve%3D1%26tl%3D1&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>Court documents from a federal conspiracy prosecution in Minnesota reveal that Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) and Homeland Security Investigations (HSI) have conducted a sweeping domestic surveillance operation targeting left-wing activists, labor unions, and community response networks. Initiated under National Security Presidential Memorandum 7 and funded through programs like Operation Keyhole, the multi-agency campaign utilized undercover agents, drones, and invasive digital tools typically reserved for counterterrorism and international crime.</p><p>Defense attorneys and former law enforcement officials compare the directive to the FBI’s historic COINTELPRO operations, noting the vast scale of data collection involving intercepted encrypted chats, financial records, and commercial data brokers. The filings indicate that the surveillance extended far beyond Minnesota to activists and conferences in cities across the United States as part of a broader federal crackdown on opponents of the administration's immigration policies.</p>

<h2>Apple Developer Tools on macOS Still Trust Compromised eMudhra Certificates</h2>
<a name="story-25676"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fdeveloper.apple.com%2Fforums%2Fcontent%2Fattachment%2Fff2cd813-bb43-4cae-8984-e40beed9a952&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>Developer tools bundled with macOS—including git, curl, and python3—continue to trust compromised eMudhra intermediate certificates more than a week after Apple was notified of the security incident. Additionally, the recent release of macOS 27.2 failed to address the issue and overwrote manual workarounds applied by users, leaving development environments vulnerable to man-in-the-middle attacks.</p><p>This trust flaw allows attackers to intercept HTTPS connections, tamper with repository clones and pushes, and capture sensitive credentials like GitHub tokens. While Apple has secured Safari and applications using its core networking framework, developers must manually remove the affected root certificates and reapply fixes after system updates until an official patch is issued.</p>

<p>In an interview with IEEE Spectrum, WikiProject AI Cleanup founding member Ellia Lebleu discusses the volunteer efforts launched in late 2023 to detect and remove artificial intelligence-generated material from Wikipedia. The project relies on developing detection tools, automated verifiers, and coordination with the Wikimedia Foundation, raising broader questions about maintaining Wikipedia as a uniquely human-written corpus in an era dominated by generative AI.</p>

<h2>Orestis Ioannou on How AI Coding Agents Increased His Workload</h2>
<a name="story-25575"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Foioannou.com%2Fimg%2Fai-working-more%2Fcontributions-per-month.svg&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>Full-stack engineer and tech executive Orestis Ioannou analyzes his GitHub contribution history to examine how adopting parallel AI coding agents significantly increased his workload rather than reducing it. Despite transitioning to leadership roles with less direct coding responsibility, utilizing multiple agents concurrently allowed him to pursue vastly more projects, returning his output to peak engineering levels.</p><p>The analysis highlights that low-cost implementation drives continuous cycles of intense coding bursts, context-switching fatigue, and burnout, while elevating baseline activity during quieter periods. Ioannou notes that while AI makes turning ideas into reality easier, human cognitive capacity for managing parallel workflows has not scaled at the same rate.</p>

<h2>Open3DLab Reports Roblox Scraper Downloaded 145 GB of Community 3D Models</h2>
<a name="story-25570"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fpreview.free3d.com%2Fimg%2F2015%2F06%2F2206023501305873745%2Fkpjijuti.jpg&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>In mid-October 2026, a web crawler identified with Roblox research downloaded 145.5 GB of 3D models across 712 projects from Open3DLab and its sister sites, SFMLab and SmutBase. The scraping script ignored robots.txt instructions, terms of service, and non-commercial creator licenses while gathering data for what operators suspect is 3D foundation model training.</p><p>The downloads included significant volumes of explicit adult content from SmutBase and SFMLab, raising concerns about safety filters and data hygiene for generative AI tools used on platforms with minor users. Open3DLab stated that Roblox failed to respond to multiple inquiries regarding the automated collection, underscoring ongoing tensions over unauthorized web scraping by major technology companies.</p>
