<h2>DJW Audio Releases Free DW-303 Bass Synthesizer Plugin</h2>
<p>DJW Audio has released DW-303, a free, open-source TB-303-style bass synthesizer and step sequencer available for macOS, Windows, and Linux. The instrument models the original hardware's oscillator, filter, envelopes, accent, and slide circuits based on schematic analysis and hardware measurements.</p><p>The plugin features a 16-step hardware-style sequencer supporting pattern chaining, MIDI clock synchronization, and a standalone application mode that accepts MIDI input. It is distributed in VST3, CLAP, AU, AAX, and LV2 formats across supported platforms, requiring no license key or activation.</p>

<h2>Never Found Audio Releases MG-Mini Free MIDI Grabber Plugin</h2>
<p>Never Found Audio has released MG-Mini, a free MIDI grabber plugin for Windows. Available in VST3 and Standalone formats, the plugin captures audio input in the background and converts it to MIDI data via a "Grab" button. It supports both single-note (Single Line) and chord-based (Full Stack) modes, and users can drag audio files directly into the interface for conversion.</p><p>The lightweight tool is designed for Windows 10 or later, though a macOS version is currently unavailable. To access the software, users must provide an email address to receive the download link. The developer intentionally omitted features such as note editing and harmonic analysis to maintain a small software footprint.</p>

<h2>BPB Reader Poll Reveals Attitudes Toward AI in Music Software</h2>
<a name="story-26924"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fblog-uploads.imgix.net%2F2026%2F01%2Fai-tools-music-producers-actually-use-2026.png%3Fauto%3Dcompress%2Cformat%26w%3D1072&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>A poll of over 2,000 Bedroom Producers Blog (BPB) readers shows a preference for AI tools designed for specific technical tasks—such as stem separation, noise removal, and code assistance—while expressing strong opposition to AI music generators. Approximately 89% of respondents support AI-related coverage, but 88% rejected tools that generate finished songs from text prompts.</p><p>The results highlight concerns regarding software quality, maintenance, and the ethical use of training data. While some producers utilize AI for specific workflow benefits or accessibility, the consensus favors tools that assist the creative process rather than replacing the musician.</p>
