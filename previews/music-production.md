<h2>Born Second Releases TimeTravel Free Reverse Delay Plugin</h2>
<a name="story-25582"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fmedia.plugins4free.com%2Fimg%2FdualReverseDelay_2.jpg&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>Born Second has released TimeTravel, a free reverse-delay plugin for macOS, Windows, and Linux. Modeled after the Old Blood Noise Endeavors BL-44 Reverse pedal, the effect captures incoming audio using a fixed memory buffer and a 10-bit converter before playing it back in reverse.</p><p>The plugin includes controls for Clock, Speed, Mix, and Volume, alongside a bypass switch. Adjusting the Clock knob simultaneously alters the sample rate, filter cutoff, and capture window length from 0.4 to 2.4 seconds, while the Speed knob shifts playback pitch across semitones with a tape-like glide.</p>

<h2>ebbandflow Releases deCADE Vintage Synth Instrument</h2>
<a name="story-25583"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.audiopluginguy.com%2Fwp-content%2Fuploads%2FParty-Bag-Plugins-releases-Reel-Synth-vintage-synthesizer-plugin-and-standalone-app-2048x1072.png&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>ebbandflow has released deCADE, a sample-based virtual instrument for macOS and Windows that combines 127 individual captures from 1980s synthesizers into 100 layered sound sources. Recorded in Scottish studios, the instrument features classic hardware models such as the Roland Jupiter-6, Prophet-5, and Yamaha DX7, paired together to create dual-synth patches.</p><p>Building on the framework of the earlier deFORM plugin, deCADE includes a radial pad for independently adjusting voice stacking and layer blending. It offers over 200 factory presets, flexible filters, polyphonic glide, and vintage-modeled effects including a Juno-style chorus and tape-style delay. Available in VST, AU, and AAX formats, the instrument supports MPE and requires 14 GB of storage space.</p>

<h2>AcusMagic Releases Figura Real-Time Cymatics Visualizer Plugin</h2>
<a name="story-25537"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fstatic.kvraudio.com%2Fi%2Fb%2Ffigura-ui-sand-01---01.jpg&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>AcusMagic has released Figura, an audio effect and standalone application that translates incoming audio signals into real-time cymatic figures. Utilizing modal simulation rather than simple beat-reactive animation, the software drives the eigenmodes of virtual plates and membranes using up to 32 partials of the sound source, forming Chladni patterns, liquid waves, and Lissajous curves.</p><p>Available in Studio and Live editions for macOS and Windows, Figura supports VST3, CLAP, AU, and AAX formats alongside standalone operation. The Live version features a dedicated projection window for external monitors, and the software allows users to export still images up to 4096 pixels and MP4 video recordings up to 4K resolution.</p>

<h2>M-Vave FM-1 Pocket Synthesizer Emerges as Custom Firmware Platform</h2>
<a name="story-25506"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fm.media-amazon.com%2Fimages%2FI%2F61gJBdDcHuL._AC_SL1200_.jpg&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>The M-Vave FM-1 is an inexpensive pocket frequency-modulation synthesizer that has evolved into a popular platform for community-developed custom firmware. Originally released as a budget device inspired by the Yamaha DX7, the hardware sparked reverse-engineering efforts that led to user-created software expanding its capabilities far beyond its stock configuration.</p><p>In response to the community trend, manufacturer M-Vave launched FM1 Open, an open-source development package featuring a browser-based workbench, software development kit, and updated firmware options. Community developers have since ported a wide variety of alternate packages to the device, transforming the pocket synthesizer into a multi-engine groovebox, chiptune workstation, and even a platform capable of running Doom.</p>
