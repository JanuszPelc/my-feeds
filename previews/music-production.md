<h2>Open Firmware and Community Development Gaining Traction in Electronic Music Gear</h2>
<a name="story-25916"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.synthtopia.com%2Fwp-content%2Fuploads%2F2026%2F10%2Fm-wave-fm-1-1.jpg&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>Open-source firmware and community-developed software are increasingly shaping the electronic music hardware market, spurred by the success of affordable, modifiable instruments like the M-Vave FM-1 and new developer support from established manufacturers.</p><p>Companies such as Akai, Behringer, and Erica Synths are introducing open firmware initiatives or software development kits for devices like the KICK and LXR-02, while independent projects like DOOM OS for Roland samplers demonstrate strong user demand for custom workflows. Although AI-assisted coding tools are accelerating community development, the trend also raises industry questions regarding long-term support, device stability, and security risks from unofficial software modifications.</p>

<h2>Daniel Fernandes Releases Free Spotlight Stereo Shaping Plugin</h2>
<a name="story-25853"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.image-line.com%2Ffl-studio-learning%2Ffl-studio-online-manual%2Fhtml%2Fimg_plug%2Fstereoshaper_tutorial5.jpg&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>Developer Daniel Fernandes has released Spotlight, a free, pay-what-you-want stereo shaping plugin currently in beta for Windows, Linux, and macOS. The plugin features a minimalist interface that allows users to adjust both a sound's stereo width and panning simultaneously by dragging a single dot across a semicircular surface.</p><p>Users can refine their settings by scrolling to adjust stereo focus independently or right-clicking and dragging to alter the pan, while a Ctrl-click resets both parameters. <h2>Moog Releases Model 112 and Model 210 FRFR Amplifiers for Electronic Instruments</h2>
<a name="story-25833"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fmusictech.com%2Fwp-content%2Fuploads%2F2026%2F10%2FMoog-Model-112-and-Moog-Model-210.jpeg&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>Moog Music has launched the Model 112 and Model 210, a pair of full-range, flat-response (FRFR) amplifiers designed specifically for synthesizers, keyboards, drum machines, and electronic performance setups. Built to preserve the authentic sonic character of electronic instruments rather than coloring the sound, the amplifiers feature integrated four-channel mixers and versatile connectivity options for studio and live environments.</p><p>The compact Model 112 costs $799 and features a 12-inch woofer, a 1-inch compression driver, and 440W RMS of bi-amplified power. The larger Model 210 is priced at $999, offering 550W RMS power, dual 10-inch woofers, and a 2.5-way design that extends low frequencies down to 35Hz. Both models include Bluetooth streaming, headphone monitoring, and a Stereo Link function for connecting dual-amplifier setups.</p>
