<h2>Maynard v. Snapchat Lawsuit and the Legal Reckoning Over Social Media User Safety</h2>
<a name="story-25781"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Ftrulaw.com%2Fwp-content%2Fuploads%2F2024%2F02%2FOverview-of-the-Snapchat-Lawsuit-1.webp&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>The 2016 car crash in Georgia involving a driver using Snapchat's "Speed Filter" sparked the lawsuit Maynard v. Snapchat, challenging liability protections for social media platforms. The litigation focused on product design flaws that allegedly encouraged dangerous behavior rather than third-party content hosted by the service.</p><p>By framing the claims around negligent feature design rather than speech publication, the lawsuit bypassed standard Section 230 immunity barriers. The legal battle prompted heightened scrutiny of feature-driven engagement models and led Snapchat to retire the speed filter amid mounting safety concerns.</p>

<p>Zillow has agreed to pay $212.5 million in cash to settle a securities class action lawsuit accusing the company of misleading shareholders regarding its defunct Zillow Offers home-flipping venture. The resolution puts to rest years of legal battles led by investor-rights attorneys on behalf of shareholders who suffered financial declines following the unexpected termination of the real estate venture.</p>

<h2>New York Governor Kathy Hochul Signs AI Chatbot and Baby Monitor Safety Bills</h2>
<a name="story-25738"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.babymonitor101.com%2Fwp-content%2Fuploads%2F2025%2F04%2Fessential-security-features-look-when.jpg0513.jpg&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>New York Governor Kathy Hochul signed two laws aimed at protecting children from risks associated with artificial intelligence and connected devices. One bill prohibits AI-powered chatbots from using "unsafe features" when interacting with minors, such as those that are sexually explicit, encourage self-harm, or mimic human personas. The second law requires internet-connected baby monitors to include security features that prevent unauthorized remote access or listening.</p><p>The chatbot regulations, which require age verification and grant the state Attorney General the authority to seek civil penalties of up to $25,000 per violation, are expected to take effect in the coming months. The baby monitor requirements will be implemented within 90 days. These measures are a key part of Hochul's reelection campaign platform, which emphasizes the regulation of social media and emerging technologies.</p>

<h2>University of Kentucky Research Administrator Granted Religious Exemption From AI Use</h2>
<a name="story-25725"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.nisarlaw.com%2Fwp-content%2Fuploads%2F2025%2F12%2FReligious-Accommodation-Process-1024x833.png&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>University of Kentucky research administrator David Durman has become the first humanist to receive a Title VII religious accommodation excusing him from personally using artificial intelligence tools at work. Assisted by the American Humanist Association, Durman argued that offloading cognitive and moral reasoning to AI platforms like ChatGPT and Claude violates his humanist commitment to personal responsibility and human flourishing.</p><p>Federal courts recognize humanism as a qualifying belief system, affording adherents protections equivalent to traditional religions under federal law. Durman's case follows a similar exemption secured by a Unitarian Universalist software engineer and arrives amid growing legal and ethical discussions regarding workplace AI mandates and broader societal skepticism toward automated technologies.</p>

<h2>ICE and HSI Conduct Sweeping Surveillance Operation Targeting Left-Wing Groups</h2>
<a name="story-25693"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fa57.foxnews.com%2Fstatic.foxnews.com%2Ffoxnews.com%2Fcontent%2Fuploads%2F2026%2F02%2F1200%2F675%2Fmn-ice-plates-database-homeland-security-investigations.jpg%3Fve%3D1%26tl%3D1&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>Court documents from a federal conspiracy prosecution in Minnesota reveal that Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) and Homeland Security Investigations (HSI) have conducted a sweeping domestic surveillance operation targeting left-wing activists, labor unions, and community response networks. Initiated under National Security Presidential Memorandum 7 and funded through programs like Operation Keyhole, the multi-agency campaign utilized undercover agents, drones, and invasive digital tools typically reserved for counterterrorism and international crime.</p><p>Defense attorneys and former law enforcement officials compare the directive to the FBI’s historic COINTELPRO operations, noting the vast scale of data collection involving intercepted encrypted chats, financial records, and commercial data brokers. The filings indicate that the surveillance extended far beyond Minnesota to activists and conferences in cities across the United States as part of a broader federal crackdown on opponents of the administration's immigration policies.</p>

<h2>CFTC Sues Utah to Protect Federal Oversight of Kalshi Prediction Markets</h2>
<a name="story-25657"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fs3-images.ctmedia.io%2Fmedia%2Fcontent%2F019ce0f5-fdcf-77f7-b5b8-c2cdf918d17a.png&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>The U.S. Commodity Futures Trading Commission has filed a federal lawsuit against Utah to block the state's enforcement actions against prediction market platform Kalshi. The federal agency argues that its regulatory authority over event contracts preempts state laws that ban gambling and restrict Kalshi's operations.</p><p>The legal challenge marks the tenth time the CFTC has taken a state to court to defend its position as the sole regulator of prediction markets. Federal appellate courts have issued conflicting rulings on whether sports and event betting falls under federal jurisdiction or state control, leaving the legal framework deeply divided.</p>
