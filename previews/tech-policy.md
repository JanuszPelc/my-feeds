<h2>Jakub T. Jankiewicz Argues All AI-Generated Code Should Be Open Source</h2>
<a name="story-27573"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fres.cloudinary.com%2Fdyd911kmh%2Fimage%2Fupload%2Fv1676548360%2FOpen_AI_Products_618960c677.png&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>Software developer Jakub T. Jankiewicz argues that all code generated by artificial intelligence should be released as open source. He contends that because AI models are trained on scraped open-source repositories and crowd-sourced platforms like Stack Overflow without proper attribution, withholding the resulting output as proprietary software amounts to license laundering.</p><p>Jankiewicz also points to legal precedents, noting that purely AI-generated works lack human authorship and therefore generally cannot be copyrighted, as established by digital copyright disputes and policies such as Wikimedia Commons' public domain tagging for AI media. He suggests that releasing AI-assisted code under open-source licenses or Creative Commons Zero addresses international legal discrepancies and honors the collaborative foundations of modern software development.</p>
