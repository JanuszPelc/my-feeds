<h2>AI Regulation Could Benefit from Safety Frameworks Used in High-Hazard Industries</h2>
<a name="story-27772"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fimages.theconversation.com%2Ffiles%2F770360%2Foriginal%2Ffile-20261105-64-ft00t4.jpg%3Fixlib%3Drb-4.1.1%26q%3D45%26auto%3Dformat%26w%3D754%26fit%3Dclip&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>AI regulation faces significant challenges due to the increasing complexity and autonomy of AI agents. In highly interconnected systems, the interaction of multiple components can trigger unpredictable, cascading failures—a phenomenon known as "normal accidents"—that may occur too rapidly for human intervention.</p><p>Adopting frameworks from high-reliability industries like aviation and nuclear power could help manage these risks. By focusing on organizational safety cultures, continuous learning from near misses, and robust assurance infrastructures, regulators can move beyond one-off technical tests toward a more resilient governance model.</p>

<h2>Strict Liability Examined as Legal Standard for Artificial Intelligence Regulation</h2>
<a name="story-27738"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fwcr.legal%2Fwp-content%2Fuploads%2F2026%2F05%2FEU-AI-LIABILITY-WHAT-RULES-APPLY-NOW.png&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>Business law professor Deven Desai argues that applying strict liability and strict product liability principles can help hold artificial intelligence companies accountable for harms caused by their software. As lawsuits against developers and social media platforms mount, shifting legal responsibility away from users and onto creators can provide a vital deterrent and ensure proper compensation for victims.</p><p>The analysis explores two main tort law doctrines: treating advanced AI systems as abnormally dangerous activities similar to handling explosives, and holding companies liable for design defects such as a lack of safety guardrails in chatbots. Although the continuous evolution and deep integration of AI into daily technology pose unique legal hurdles, advocates contend that strict rules are necessary to shift the risks of emerging technologies from the public to the companies profiting from them.</p>
