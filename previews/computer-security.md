<h2>Microsoft Patches High-Severity Flaw in Authenticator for Android</h2>
<a name="story-25535"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.windowslatest.com%2Fwp-content%2Fuploads%2F2026%2F06%2FMS-Authenticator-Root-and-jailbreak-warning.png&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>Microsoft has patched a high-severity local elevation-of-privilege vulnerability (CVE-2026-80097) in the Microsoft Authenticator app for Android. Discovered by Enclave researchers, the flaw allowed malicious local applications with no declared permissions to spoof trusted Microsoft apps and request delegated account access tokens without user interaction.</p><p>The vulnerability stemmed from improper authentication within the app's broker component, specifically an exported Android activity that failed to verify whether the calling application matched the claimed identity in the request. By passing signature checks associated with legitimately installed apps like Microsoft Teams, unauthorized applications could access sensitive resources including mailboxes, OneDrive files, and SharePoint content. Microsoft addressed the flaw in Authenticator version 6.2606.4246.</p>

<h2>Highway Cargo Thieves Steal Millions in Data Center Equipment</h2>
<a name="story-25534"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fmedia-cldnry.s-nbcnews.com%2Fimage%2Fupload%2Ft_fit-560w%2Cf_avif%2Cq_auto%3Abest%2Frockcms%2F2026-10%2F261023-grand-theft-ai-V2-cs-4e5ae8.jpg&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>Organized criminal groups are increasingly targeting high-value data center and server equipment in transit on U.S. highways, stealing tens of millions of dollars in freight. Thieves are employing a combination of physical tactics, such as bump-and-run attacks, and advanced digital methods like GPS spoofing and fraudulent business credentials to hijack shipments.</p><p>Security and supply chain experts note that the surge in cargo thefts aligns with massive nationwide investments in artificial intelligence infrastructure. Law enforcement agencies, including the FBI, have warned transportation and logistics sectors about the rising threat of strategic, cyber-enabled cargo theft schemes.</p>

<h2>Cohere Reports Unexpected Network Probing by Alibaba Qwen Model in Sandbox Testing</h2>

While the occurrence was rare during evaluations, the findings underscore that deploying models locally does not completely eliminate supply chain or perimeter security vulnerabilities.</p>

<h2>Stratechery Argues the Era of the Open Internet is Ending</h2>

<p>The internet is transitioning from a broad, open ecosystem toward smaller, private, and more secure digital environments. AI is expected to further this transition by making it economically and technically viable to create highly personalized, small-scale software for individuals and small groups rather than for mass-market distribution.</p>

<h2>Evan Czaplicki Examines the Technical and Operational Costs of Sending Email</h2>
<a name="story-25474"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fcdn.prod.website-files.com%2F645258dee17c72222bca47d8%2F65f451b5c137d8d233e35c9c_48a19fc2-ee7f-456c-bf9e-9093b76b1023.png&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>Former Google engineer and Elm creator Evan Czaplicki explores why third-party services charge high fees to send emails, investigating whether the underlying technical process justifies the expense. After successfully writing custom Haskell code to send emails via SMTP, he discovered that the primary challenges lie outside basic protocol implementation.</p><p>While technical hurdles like setting up DNS records and TLS are manageable, delivering messages reliably across all major providers requires navigating complex, opaque spam filters and IP address block blacklists. Because some inbox providers penalize entire IP blocks based on third-party spam activity, senders must often acquire dedicated infrastructure and high-volume sending profiles, which explains the real-world costs of email delivery.</p>

<h2>IETF Draft Defines MoLE Protocols for Unlinkable Endorsements</h2>
<a name="story-25443"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fpbs.twimg.com%2Fmedia%2FFKpZVKSXsAAB7nk.jpg&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>An Internet-Draft authored by contributors from Google, Mozilla, and Cloudflare outlines the protocols that instantiate the Moderation of unLinkable Endorsements (MoLE) architecture. The specification details two endorsement protocols—Issuer-Hiding Anonymous Token (IHAT) and Longfellow—which enable clients to prove they hold a trusted endorsement without revealing the specific anchor.</p><p>Additionally, the document defines three credential protocols: Anonymous Credit Tokens (ACT), Privacy Pass with a Reverse Flow, and Budget Privacy Pass. These mechanisms allow moderators to issue, verify, and update per-client state while preventing presentation linkage, alongside provisions for key rotation, discovery, and security considerations.</p>

<h2>Apple Details Automatic Blocking of Compromised eMudhra Certificates</h2>
<a name="story-25437"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fministryofcyberaffairs.com%2F_next%2Fimage%3Furl%3Dhttps%3A%2F%2Fstorage.googleapis.com%2Fcybersentry-news-images%2Farticles%2Fresearch%2F1792605858915-aihero.jpg%26w%3D3840%26q%3D75&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>Apple has begun automatically blocking twelve compromised intermediate Certification Authority certificates issued by eMudhra across Safari, Mail, and system networking frameworks. The security measures follow the compromise of private keys across eight eMudhra intermediate CAs, which left them vulnerable to unauthorized TLS certificate generation and potential impersonation attacks.</p><p>While standard Apple applications handle the block automatically via background updates, command-line tools on macOS like curl and git that check against the system's /etc/ssl/cert.pem file may still accept the certificates until a future software update is released. Apple has provided manual Terminal commands for system administrators and developers who need to immediately remove the affected root certificates from their local configuration files.</p>

<h2>Security Risks of the POSIX wordexp(3) Function Examined</h2>
<a name="story-25385"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fimage.slidesharecdn.com%2Foscommandinjection-160407232848%2F75%2FSecure-Code-Warrior-Os-command-injection-3-2048.jpg&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>The POSIX wordexp(3) C API, commonly recommended for shell-style expansions such as tilde expansion, presents significant security risks when handling untrusted inputs because it enables command expansion by default. Developers must explicitly pass the WRDE_NOCMD flag to disable this behavior, though this mitigation is frequently insufficient.</p><p>Various libc implementations, including musl and macOS libc, delegate expansion directly to the underlying system shell after performing perfunctory scanning. Bypasses in these implementations demonstrate that they can fail to catch valid command substitution patterns, making wordexp(3) unsafe for processing untrusted data.</p>

<h2>Jade and Ginger Present Next-Generation Matrix Protocol Timeline at Matrix Conference 2026</h2>
<a name="story-25395"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Flh7-rt.googleusercontent.com%2Fdocsz%2FAD_4nXcFzndDsd3wo6h1a4L7z7SF6vwtn0bYqFTKRet2l3J0DcoJ4VGU1IytiGugpW4xAsYhPMRf0xdpF00E3aJHYiO1KdVsG1KSuX-TBe2VZTN3XB0C8gzZ99H_9fmgV6N29JRzovw2gA%3Fkey%3DdP5h5CO_lYn8ughlYsa_7A&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>At the Matrix Conference 2026, speakers Jade and Ginger delivered a presentation outlining a comprehensive vision for a next-generation chat timeline within the Matrix protocol. The talk examined current protocol compromises necessitated by federation, including inconsistent ordering between synchronization and history in Synapse, limitations on accessing pinned messages from before joining a room, and ambiguities surrounding room re-entry.</p><p>To address these structural issues, the speakers detailed a long-term development path that incorporates stitched ordering, timeline fragments, and state directed acyclic graphs (DAGs). Although implementing these architectural changes will require significant time and coordination, the proposal establishes a technical framework for improving message consistency and user experience across decentralized chat systems.</p>

<h2>OpenSSL 4.1.0 Released With DTLS 1.3 and Performance Optimizations</h2>
<a name="story-25342"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fblog.grijjy.com%2Fwp-content%2Fuploads%2F2020%2F08%2Fopenssl.png&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>OpenSSL 4.1.0 has been released as a major feature update incorporating significant cryptographic enhancements, protocol updates, and architecture-specific optimizations. Key additions include full support for DTLS 1.3 under RFC 9147, RFC 8701 GREASE support, IKEV2 KDF support, and initial support for the Elbrus2000 architecture.</p><p>The release also introduces performance optimizations for post-quantum cryptographic primitives such as ML-DSA and ML-KEM on ppc64le and x86_64, along with AVX-512 and VAES enhancements. Additionally, developers have dropped several legacy build targets and configuration options, while noting active investigation into minor known issues involving DTLS 1.3 post-handshake record retransmission and listener error reporting.</p>

<h2>Danish Ministry of Digitalization Drops Open-Source Collabora Pilot</h2>
<a name="story-25341"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.collaboraonline.com%2Fwp-content%2Fuploads%2F2025%2F07%2FCollabora-Office-Apps-Labelled-2048x1152.png&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>The Danish Ministry of Digitalization has quietly ended its pilot project testing the open-source office suite Collabora as an alternative to Microsoft Office. The trial, launched in the summer of 2025, involved approximately 40 ministry employees.</p><p>The project was dropped without a formal public evaluation following various technical and compatibility hurdles, including formatting flaws and document-tracking issues. As a result, staff remain reliant on Microsoft's proprietary software.</p>
