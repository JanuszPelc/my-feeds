<h2>Astronomer Nikhita Ramkilowan Explains What Would Happen If Earth Stopped Spinning</h2>

<p>Astronomer Nikhita Ramkilowan explores the hypothetical scenario of Earth abruptly or gradually stopping its rotation, examining the dramatic environmental and biological consequences that would follow. If the planet ceased spinning, one hemisphere would experience perpetual scorching sunlight while the other plunged into freezing darkness, creating extreme temperature gradients and permanent global weather disruption.</p><p>Life on Earth depends heavily on the predictable 24-hour cycle of day and night for survival, internal clocks, and navigation, meaning most ecosystems would struggle under static conditions. Additionally, a non-spinning planet could weaken Earth's invisible magnetic shield generated by its molten iron core, while requiring significant redesigns for satellite and communication technology. However, scientists confirm that Earth is in no danger of stopping, as it is only slowing down by an imperceptible 1.7 milliseconds every century.</p>

<p>Human movement and equipment use in Antarctica's McMurdo Dry Valleys redistribute locally adapted microbial communities between sites. Because many of these microbes have evolved into unique strains found nowhere else on Earth, their unintended redistribution via human activity poses a risk to these localized ecosystems. These findings underscore the need for enhanced biosecurity monitoring and stricter decontamination procedures for Antarctic field operations.</p>
