<h2>Excavations at Oued Beht Reveal Bronze Age Defensive Wall in Morocco</h2>
<a name="story-26032"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fnuttersworld.com%2Fancient-trade-routes-mediterranean-sea%2Fmorocco-to-iberia%2Foued-beht1.webp&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>Recent excavations at the Oued Beht archaeological site in north-west Morocco have uncovered a large-scale defensive wall dating back roughly 4,000 to 3,500 years. Described in a new study published in Libyan Studies, the multi-phase ditch, rampart, and wall system represents the earliest known large-scale defensive construction in North Africa outside the Nile Valley.</p><p>The structure indicates that Bronze Age communities in the Maghreb were increasingly organized and capable of coordinating complex labor, likely responding to competition over land and resources. Furthermore, evidence from the site points to active connections with broader western Mediterranean networks, while subsequent medieval remains demonstrate that the ridge was utilized again centuries later.</p>

<h2>Niobium-Titanium Alloys Maintain Ductility at Extreme Cryogenic Temperatures</h2>
<a name="story-26031"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.samaterials.com%2Fimages%2Fsc%2F1749459775-normal-BG00414.jpg&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>Researchers at the University of California, Berkeley, Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, and the University of Oxford have discovered that two experimental niobium-titanium metal alloys resist brittle failure at temperatures down to 20 kelvin (-253°C or -424°F). Publishing their findings in Nature, the team observed that these body-centered cubic alloys avoid the severe ductile-to-brittle transition that typically causes such materials to fracture under extreme cold.</p><p>Rather than relying on traditional dislocation movement, which becomes restricted at extremely low temperatures, the alloys activate nanoscale twinning and local phase transformations to generate new structural defects that accommodate stress. This mechanism provides a new design framework for engineering structural metals capable of operating in demanding environments across aerospace, energy systems, and cryogenic applications.</p>

<h2>Shanidar Cave Neanderthal Fossil Shows Evidence of Fatal Wooden Spear Wound</h2>

<p>An analysis of a 75,000-year-old Neanderthal shoulder blade from Shanidar Cave in Iraqi Kurdistan suggests the individual was killed by a sharpened wooden spear. Researchers identified a precise circular opening in the delicate scapula bone that matches wounds created by wooden projectile or thrusting weapons, potentially pointing to an intentional attack directed at the heart.</p><p>Despite the violent nature of the death, other skeletal remains from the roughly 45-year-old individual, designated Shanidar A, reveal signs of surviving a severe earlier facial trauma for at least a year, indicating long-term care and social support from other members of the group. The findings offer rare physical evidence of both interpersonal violence and community care within Neanderthal society.</p>

<h2>STAR Experiment at RHIC Detects Quark-Gluon Plasma in Oxygen Collisions</h2>
<a name="story-26030"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.energy.gov%2Fsites%2Fdefault%2Ffiles%2Fstyles%2Ffull_article_width%2Fpublic%2F2025-12%2F121125-blog-rhic-history-3.jpg%3Fitok%3DXVNldgFY&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>Researchers analyzing data from the STAR experiment at the Relativistic Heavy Ion Collider (RHIC) have found strong evidence of quark-gluon plasma—a primordial state of matter from the early universe—produced by colliding oxygen nuclei. By measuring high-energy particle streams, the team observed a 20 percent suppression of particles, known as jet quenching, indicating that the dense plasma forms even in smaller and lower-energy collision systems than previously demonstrated.</p><p>While quark-gluon plasma has traditionally been studied using massive gold nuclei or at higher energies at CERN's Large Hadron Collider, the new RHIC findings establish a lower boundary for the conditions required to create this early-universe matter. The measurements, conducted using oxygen data collected before RHIC concluded operations in early 2026, help physicists better understand the minimal thresholds necessary for primordial matter to emerge.</p>

<p>Researchers at University College London have found that crocodiles and their ancient relatives survived the asteroid impact 66 million years ago largely due to a long-standing preference for warm environments. By analyzing more than 7,000 fossils alongside computer climate models spanning 245 million years, the team determined that equatorial regions remained climatically suitable for these animals while habitats for non-avian dinosaurs sharply contracted.</p><p>The study, published in Science Advances, highlights that the broader crocodile lineage historically tracked warm conditions by expanding ranges during hot periods and retreating toward the tropics during cooling phases. The authors note that while this behavioral consistency helped them endure past mass extinctions, modern pressures such as habitat fragmentation and urbanization may restrict the ability of contemporary species to shift ranges in response to current climate change.</p>

<h2>Hubble Space Telescope Observations Challenge Supernova Wind Theory of Cosmic Reionization</h2>

<p>Observations using the Hubble Space Telescope of nearby starburst galaxies challenge the leading theory that supernova-driven galactic winds helped ultraviolet radiation escape during the universe's reionization period. These results indicate that cosmological hydrodynamic simulations, which heavily rely on supernova feedback to model how the early universe became transparent to ultraviolet light, may need reevaluation.</p>

<h2>New Seahorse-Like Fish Genus Australyichthys Described from Museum Specimens</h2>
<a name="story-25936"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.sciencedaily.com%2Fimages%2F1920%2Faustralyichthys-aegis.webp&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>Researchers have described a new genus of small, seahorse-like fish named Australyichthys based on museum specimens collected off southern Australia over the past six decades. Initially misidentified as true seahorses due to convergent evolution, the three species—including the helmethead sea muppet—lack the characteristic internal bony architecture of seahorses and belong to a distinct evolutionary lineage.</p><p>Because all known specimens were accidentally captured by trawl nets or dredges between 50 and 125 meters deep and preserved without ever being observed alive, the genus is classified as a lost species group. Micro-CT scans and DNA sequencing from ethanol-preserved tissue confirmed their unique skeletal arrangement and phylogenetic placement outside the seahorse genus Hippocampus, highlighting the unexplored biodiversity of Australia's deep temperate reefs.</p>

<h2>70 Ophiuchi AB Binary System Lacks Giant Planets, Century-Long Study Finds</h2>
<a name="story-25934"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fassignmentpoint.com%2Fwp-content%2Fuploads%2F2021%2F06%2F70-Ophiuchi-1.jpg&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>Researchers analyzing a century of data on the nearby binary star system 70 Ophiuchi AB have found no evidence of giant planets as massive as Jupiter or larger within the regions examined. Led by the University of Michigan, the study combined historical measurements with new observations from the Magellan Clay and Harlan J. Smith telescopes to re-evaluate a stellar wobble first noted in 1855.</p><p>The findings indicate that stellar surface activity, rather than the gravitational pull of a giant planet, caused the mysterious radial-velocity variations. While ruling out massive planets in the inner regions, the results help astronomers refine search strategies for smaller, potentially habitable rocky worlds in the system located 16 light-years from Earth.</p>
