<h2>Plasma-Processing Technology Extracts Oxygen From Lunar Soil</h2>
<a name="story-28110"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fimages.theconversation.com%2Ffiles%2F770640%2Foriginal%2Ffile-20261108-64-ke9kwg.jpeg%3Fixlib%3Drb-4.1.1%26q%3D45%26auto%3Dformat%26w%3D754%26fit%3Dclip&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>Researchers have demonstrated a method for extracting oxygen from lunar soil simulants using plasma-processing technology, a technique commonly employed in semiconductor manufacturing. By exposing artificial regolith to plasmas generated from industrial gases, the study showed that oxygen and oxygen-containing gases can be released from minerals. This approach offers a lower-temperature alternative to high-energy methods for breaking the chemical bonds within lunar minerals.</p><p>The process leverages decades of industrial experience with fluorine plasmas, providing a "dry" method that avoids the logistical challenges of transporting liquid chemicals to space. While current oxygen yields must be significantly improved to support practical lunar bases, the research suggests the technology could eventually be adapted to extract other valuable lunar resources like silicon, aluminum, and iron.</p>

<h2>University of Minnesota Trial Tests Engineered Virus Treatment for Pancreatic Cancer</h2>
<a name="story-28097"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.newscientist.com%2Fwp-content%2Fuploads%2F2026%2F05%2F28111438%2FSEI_299218241.jpg%3Fw%3D840&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>Researchers at the University of Minnesota have reported that an engineered oncolytic adenovirus successfully stopped tumor growth in three pancreatic cancer patients during an initial phase 1 safety trial. The virus is genetically modified to replicate selectively inside cancer cells expressing high levels of the COX-2 enzyme, causing the infected cells to rupture and die.</p><p>The patients received injections of one-tenth of the targeted maximum dose, administered directly into their locally advanced tumors. While independent oncologists caution that these early-stage, non-randomized results require verification in larger late-stage trials, the research team plans to evaluate higher doses and combine the treatment with checkpoint inhibitors to enhance immune responses.</p>

<h2>Astronomer Nikhita Ramkilowan Explains What Would Happen If Earth Stopped Spinning</h2>

However, scientists confirm that Earth is in no danger of stopping, as it is only slowing down by an imperceptible 1.7 milliseconds every century.</p>
