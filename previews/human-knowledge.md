<h2>STAR Experiment at RHIC Detects Quark-Gluon Plasma in Oxygen Collisions</h2>
<a name="story-26030"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.energy.gov%2Fsites%2Fdefault%2Ffiles%2Fstyles%2Ffull_article_width%2Fpublic%2F2025-12%2F121125-blog-rhic-history-3.jpg%3Fitok%3DXVNldgFY&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>Researchers analyzing data from the STAR experiment at the Relativistic Heavy Ion Collider (RHIC) have found strong evidence of quark-gluon plasma—a primordial state of matter from the early universe—produced by colliding oxygen nuclei. By measuring high-energy particle streams, the team observed a 20 percent suppression of particles, known as jet quenching, indicating that the dense plasma forms even in smaller and lower-energy collision systems than previously demonstrated.</p><p>While quark-gluon plasma has traditionally been studied using massive gold nuclei or at higher energies at CERN's Large Hadron Collider, the new RHIC findings establish a lower boundary for the conditions required to create this early-universe matter. The measurements, conducted using oxygen data collected before RHIC concluded operations in early 2026, help physicists better understand the minimal thresholds necessary for primordial matter to emerge.</p>
<p><small>Links: <a href="https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/10/261030002935.htm">sciencedaily.com</a> | <a href="https://www.bnl.gov/newsroom/news.php?a=123196">source</a></small></p>

<p>Researchers have published a study in Science Advances analyzing the Fireside Database, a collection of 25,419 anonymized peer-support interactions from the Fireside Project’s Psychedelic Support Line between April 2021 and February 2026. <h2>University of Birmingham Researcher Measures Time Without External Clock Using Cold Atoms</h2>
<a name="story-25933"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Findiandefencereview.com%2Fwp-content%2Fuploads%2F2026%2F06%2Fentropic-internal-time-vs-laboratory-time-across-varying-barrier-heightsphysical-review-research-1200x727.jpg&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>University of Birmingham physicist Professor Giovanni Barontini has created a miniature universe using approximately 24,000 ultracold atoms to demonstrate that the passage of time can emerge internally from system changes rather than relying on an external clock. Published in Physical Review Research, the experiment provides empirical evidence for theoretical physics concepts suggesting that time is not a fundamental property of the universe, but rather arises from relationships among its constituent particles.</p><p>The research isolates the atomic cloud using laser beams divided into observed and unobserved regions that undergo repeated expansion and contraction cycles analogous to cosmic events. By tracking entropy changes as atoms move between regions, the system establishes an internal "entropic time" that dictates an arrow of time and can be integrated into foundational quantum equations like the Schrödinger equation.</p>

<h2>UCL Study Shows Warm Climate Preference Helped Crocodiles Survive Dinosaur Extinction</h2>
<a name="story-25935"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fi.ytimg.com%2Fvi%2FRm6flPZjVM0%2Fmaxresdefault.jpg&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>Researchers at University College London have found that crocodiles and their ancient relatives survived the asteroid impact 66 million years ago largely due to a long-standing preference for warm environments. By analyzing more than 7,000 fossils alongside computer climate models spanning 245 million years, the team determined that equatorial regions remained climatically suitable for these animals while habitats for non-avian dinosaurs sharply contracted.</p><p>The study, published in Science Advances, highlights that the broader crocodile lineage historically tracked warm conditions by expanding ranges during hot periods and retreating toward the tropics during cooling phases. The authors note that while this behavioral consistency helped them endure past mass extinctions, modern pressures such as habitat fragmentation and urbanization may restrict the ability of contemporary species to shift ranges in response to current climate change.</p>

<h2>Hubble Space Telescope Observations Challenge Supernova Wind Theory of Cosmic Reionization</h2>
<a name="story-25932"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fscx1.b-cdn.net%2Fcsz%2Fnews%2F800a%2F2026%2Fshining-new-light-on-t.jpg&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>Observations using the Hubble Space Telescope of nearby starburst galaxies challenge the leading theory that supernova-driven galactic winds helped ultraviolet radiation escape during the universe's reionization period. Researchers found that stronger and faster winds correlate with lower escape rates of ionizing radiation, indicating that stellar winds actually obstruct rather than clear pathways for light.</p><p>The findings suggest that ionizing photons escape during earlier phases of star formation before powerful supernova explosions redistribute surrounding gas and dust. These results indicate that cosmological hydrodynamic simulations, which heavily rely on supernova feedback to model how the early universe became transparent to ultraviolet light, may need reevaluation.</p>

<h2>Study Estimates Up to 513,000 Annual Snakebite Deaths Worldwide</h2>

<p>A global study published in PLOS Medicine estimates that snake envenoming causes between 274,000 and 513,000 deaths and at least 2.1 million envenomings worldwide each year. Using a geostatistical model that combines literature reviews with environmental and socioeconomic factors to adjust for severe under-reporting, the researchers concluded that the public health crisis is significantly larger than official health statistics indicate.</p><p>Sub-Saharan Africa bears the heaviest burden, accounting for 45% of global envenomings and fatalities, with rates nearly three times higher than those in south Asia. The findings also show that over 800,000 survivors suffer long-term physical disabilities annually, exacerbating poverty in vulnerable rural communities and underscoring the need for targeted antivenom stockpiling and improved healthcare access to meet World Health Organization reduction targets.</p>

<h2>New Seahorse-Like Fish Genus Australyichthys Described from Museum Specimens</h2>

<p>Researchers have described a new genus of small, seahorse-like fish named Australyichthys based on museum specimens collected off southern Australia over the past six decades. Initially misidentified as true seahorses due to convergent evolution, the three species—including the helmethead sea muppet—lack the characteristic internal bony architecture of seahorses and belong to a distinct evolutionary lineage.</p><p>Because all known specimens were accidentally captured by trawl nets or dredges between 50 and 125 meters deep and preserved without ever being observed alive, the genus is classified as a lost species group. Micro-CT scans and DNA sequencing from ethanol-preserved tissue confirmed their unique skeletal arrangement and phylogenetic placement outside the seahorse genus Hippocampus, highlighting the unexplored biodiversity of Australia's deep temperate reefs.</p>

<h2>70 Ophiuchi AB Binary System Lacks Giant Planets, Century-Long Study Finds</h2>

<p>Researchers analyzing a century of data on the nearby binary star system 70 Ophiuchi AB have found no evidence of giant planets as massive as Jupiter or larger within the regions examined. Led by the University of Michigan, the study combined historical measurements with new observations from the Magellan Clay and Harlan J. Smith telescopes to re-evaluate a stellar wobble first noted in 1855.</p><p>The findings indicate that stellar surface activity, rather than the gravitational pull of a giant planet, caused the mysterious radial-velocity variations. While ruling out massive planets in the inner regions, the results help astronomers refine search strategies for smaller, potentially habitable rocky worlds in the system located 16 light-years from Earth.</p>

<h2>Martin Fowler Publishes Essay on Uncanny Valley of LLM Voice</h2>
<a name="story-25880"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fstatic-cdn.cars24.com%2Fprod%2Fcms%2F2026%2F01%2F29%2F0066df65-477d-4821-a782-2ab1c46a8c2eIMG_20260129_145754_289.jpg&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>Martin Fowler examines the phenomenon of "LLM Voice," a distinct stylistic tone found in prose generated by large language models that triggers a textual uncanny valley and erodes reader trust. He argues that this artificial prose style is instantly recognizable to human readers, rendering automated detection tools largely unnecessary.</p><p>While advising authors to avoid using LLMs for drafting or rephrasing to preserve their authentic voice, Fowler acknowledges necessary exceptions, such as assisting individuals with Parkinson's disease or aiding non-native speakers with translation. He emphasizes that writing is fundamentally a tool for human thinking, warning that delegating prose generation to AI risks outsourcing comprehension itself.</p>

<h2>700,000-Year-Old Elephant Tooth from Gesher Benot Ya&#x27;aqov Reveals Prehistoric Landscape</h2>
<a name="story-25810"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.archaeology.wiki%2Fwp-content%2Fuploads%2F2015%2F12%2Fpalaiolithiko_seminario_20151216_prosklhsh_goren-inbar.jpg&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>Researchers analyzing a 700,000-year-old straight-tusked elephant molar and tusk fragments from the Gesher Benot Ya'aqov archaeological site in northern Israel have reconstructed the region's Pleistocene environment. Understanding these ancient habitats sheds light on how early human ancestors navigated and survived changing environmental conditions during their migrations.</p>
