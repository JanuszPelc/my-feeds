<h2>Curtin University Researchers Find Heme Signatures in 115-Million-Year-Old Dinosaur Bones</h2>
<a name="story-26660"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fcdn.sci.news%2Fimages%2Fenlarge14%2Fimage_15169e-Aratasaurus-museunacionali.jpg&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>Researchers at Curtin University and international partners have discovered fossilized red blood cells and chemical evidence of heme molecules inside the 115-million-year-old bones of the theropod dinosaur Aratasaurus museunacionali. Published in the Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, the findings provide definitive chemical confirmation of hemoglobin-derived molecules persisting across deep geological time.</p><p>The extraordinary preservation is attributed to stabilization by the mineral hausmannite and rapid burial in low-oxygen sediment. The discovery challenges long-held assumptions regarding the durability of biological molecules and opens new avenues for molecular paleontology, evolutionary physiology, and astrobiology research.</p>

<h2>NC State Researchers Identify Beetle Species Damaging Blueberry Roots</h2>
<p>Researchers at North Carolina State University have identified Prionus imbricornis, a species of longhorn beetle, as the underground pest damaging commercial blueberry roots in the state. By combining field trapping with genetic barcoding of subterranean larvae, scientists resolved a decade-old mystery regarding unidentified root-feeding insects in local blueberry fields.</p><p>While North Carolina is the first state to document P. imbricornis infesting blueberries, growers currently face a lack of approved insecticides specifically labeled to control the pest. Researchers are testing potential chemical treatments and timing application methods around the beetle's reproductive cycle to help farmers protect their crops.</p>

<h2>Magnetic Fields May Allow White Dwarfs to Exceed the Chandrasekhar Limit</h2>
<p>New simulations from the Indian Institute of Science (IISc) suggest that white dwarfs can exceed the 1.4 solar mass Chandrasekhar limit if they possess strong internal magnetic fields. As these stellar remnants accumulate matter from companion stars, increasing density strengthens their internal magnetic fields, providing the pressure necessary to counteract gravity and support masses as high as 2.4 solar masses.</p><p>This phenomenon may explain the unexpected brightness of certain Type Ia supernovae, which serve as "standardizable candles" for measuring cosmic expansion. By altering the traditional relationship between a white dwarf's mass and size, magnetic pressure offers a potential explanation for observed anomalies in stellar radii and the luminosity of exploding stars.</p>

<h2>KAIST and Georgia Tech Researchers Develop Membrane for Room-Temperature Crude Oil Separation</h2>

<p>Researchers at the Korea Advanced Institute of Science and Technology and the Georgia Institute of Technology have developed a room-temperature crude oil separation method using an inexpensive, uncoated porous polyacrylonitrile membrane. Beyond petroleum refining, the technology could potentially apply to plastic recycling pyrolysis oil purification, pharmaceutical processing, and biofuel production.</p>
