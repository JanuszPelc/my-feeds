<h2>New Seahorse-Like Fish Genus Australyichthys Described from Museum Specimens</h2>
<a name="story-25936"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.sciencedaily.com%2Fimages%2F1920%2Faustralyichthys-aegis.webp&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>Researchers have described a new genus of small, seahorse-like fish named Australyichthys based on museum specimens collected off southern Australia over the past six decades. Initially misidentified as true seahorses due to convergent evolution, the three species—including the helmethead sea muppet—lack the characteristic internal bony architecture of seahorses and belong to a distinct evolutionary lineage.</p><p>Because all known specimens were accidentally captured by trawl nets or dredges between 50 and 125 meters deep and preserved without ever being observed alive, the genus is classified as a lost species group. Micro-CT scans and DNA sequencing from ethanol-preserved tissue confirmed their unique skeletal arrangement and phylogenetic placement outside the seahorse genus Hippocampus, highlighting the unexplored biodiversity of Australia's deep temperate reefs.</p>

<h2>70 Ophiuchi AB Binary System Lacks Giant Planets, Century-Long Study Finds</h2>
<a name="story-25934"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fassignmentpoint.com%2Fwp-content%2Fuploads%2F2021%2F06%2F70-Ophiuchi-1.jpg&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>Researchers analyzing a century of data on the nearby binary star system 70 Ophiuchi AB have found no evidence of giant planets as massive as Jupiter or larger within the regions examined. Led by the University of Michigan, the study combined historical measurements with new observations from the Magellan Clay and Harlan J. Smith telescopes to re-evaluate a stellar wobble first noted in 1855.</p><p>The findings indicate that stellar surface activity, rather than the gravitational pull of a giant planet, caused the mysterious radial-velocity variations. While ruling out massive planets in the inner regions, the results help astronomers refine search strategies for smaller, potentially habitable rocky worlds in the system located 16 light-years from Earth.</p>
<p><small>Links: <a href="https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/10/261028232711.htm">sciencedaily.com</a> | <a href="https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.3847/1538-4357/ae9f52">source</a></small></p>

<h2>NASA Astronaut Anil Menon and CSA Astronaut Joshua Kutryk Complete Spacewalk to Install ISS Solar Array</h2>
<a name="story-25931"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fscitechdaily.com%2Fimages%2FAstronaut-Woody-Hoburg-IROSA-Deployment.jpg&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>NASA astronaut Anil Menon and Canadian Space Agency astronaut Joshua Kutryk completed a seven-hour, 11-minute spacewalk outside the International Space Station, installing and deploying the seventh International Space Station Roll-Out Solar Array (IROSA). The new solar array on the 2A electrical power channel provides additional electricity for station operations, including supporting the outpost's eventual safe and controlled deorbit.</p><p>The mission marked the 100th U.S. spacewalk dedicated to station assembly and maintenance, serving as Menon's fourth spacewalk and Kutryk's first. The crew also performed preparatory tasks for a second spacewalk scheduled for November 5 to install the eighth and final solar array on the 3B power channel. Upon completion of the solar power expansion, the station's electricity generation capacity will increase from 160 kilowatts to approximately 215 kilowatts, boosting available power by up to 30%.</p>

<h2>Martin Fowler Publishes Essay on Uncanny Valley of LLM Voice</h2>
<a name="story-25880"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fstatic-cdn.cars24.com%2Fprod%2Fcms%2F2026%2F01%2F29%2F0066df65-477d-4821-a782-2ab1c46a8c2eIMG_20260129_145754_289.jpg&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>Martin Fowler examines the phenomenon of "LLM Voice," a distinct stylistic tone found in prose generated by large language models that triggers a textual uncanny valley and erodes reader trust. He argues that this artificial prose style is instantly recognizable to human readers, rendering automated detection tools largely unnecessary.</p><p>While advising authors to avoid using LLMs for drafting or rephrasing to preserve their authentic voice, Fowler acknowledges necessary exceptions, such as assisting individuals with Parkinson's disease or aiding non-native speakers with translation. He emphasizes that writing is fundamentally a tool for human thinking, warning that delegating prose generation to AI risks outsourcing comprehension itself.</p>

<h2>700,000-Year-Old Elephant Tooth from Gesher Benot Ya&#x27;aqov Reveals Prehistoric Landscape</h2>
<a name="story-25810"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.archaeology.wiki%2Fwp-content%2Fuploads%2F2015%2F12%2Fpalaiolithiko_seminario_20151216_prosklhsh_goren-inbar.jpg&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>Researchers analyzing a 700,000-year-old straight-tusked elephant molar and tusk fragments from the Gesher Benot Ya'aqov archaeological site in northern Israel have reconstructed the region's Pleistocene environment. By combining CT scans, microscopic wear analysis, isotope geochemistry, and pollen studies, the team determined that the area supported a persistent landscape of woodlands, open grasslands, marshes, and flowing rivers with dependable water resources.</p><p>Located within the Levantine Corridor—a major migration route connecting Africa and Eurasia—the site provided a stable ecological refuge that sustained both giant mammals and early human populations producing Acheulian stone tools. Understanding these ancient habitats sheds light on how early human ancestors navigated and survived changing environmental conditions during their migrations.</p>

<h2>NASA Juno Spacecraft Reveals Complex Plasma Wave Structure at Jupiter&#x27;s Bow Shock</h2>
<a name="story-25809"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Ffuturism.com%2Fwp-content%2Fuploads%2F2016%2F07%2FA-NASA.jpg%3Fquality%3D85%26w%3D1024&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>Researchers analyzing data from NASA’s Juno spacecraft have mapped Jupiter’s bow shock in unprecedented detail, revealing a planetary defense system significantly more complex than Earth’s. The study shows that the solar system’s largest planet uses multi-frequency plasma waves and secondary shock structures known as shocklets to slow and heat incoming solar wind particles before they reach the primary boundary.</p><p>Published in Nature Communications, the findings demonstrate how Jupiter’s powerful magnetic environment manages high-energy solar particles differently than Earth, which relies on a simpler, single-frequency boundary. Because these plasma interactions share fundamental physics with extreme cosmic shocks found around supernova remnants, the observations provide a natural laboratory for studying energy dissipation in distant astrophysical environments.</p>

<h2>UC Davis Mathematicians Challenge Dark Energy with Study on Spacetime Instability</h2>
<a name="story-25808"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fai2-s2-public.s3.amazonaws.com%2Ffigures%2F2017-08-08%2F8640134743cb85df200a734d26f60657072772a8%2F13-Figure1-1.png&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>Researchers at the University of California, Davis, have published a study in the Proceedings of the Royal Society A suggesting that the accelerating expansion of the universe can be explained by Einstein's original equations without invoking dark energy. By analyzing the stability of Friedmann spacetimes at the Big Bang using the Einstein-Euler equations, the team found that conventional cosmological models exhibit a fundamental instability at both small and large scales.</p><p>The mathematical analysis indicates that disturbances in these standard spacetimes grow over time, implying that cosmic acceleration could emerge naturally from general relativity rather than requiring a cosmological constant. While the study provides a theoretical challenge to the Lambda-cold dark matter model, establishing whether this alternative framework reflects actual observations will require further empirical testing.</p>

<h2>Computer Analysis of the Norse Board Game Starve the Fox</h2>
<a name="story-25807"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fentropicthoughts.com%2Fimage%2Fwhat-the-fox-say_01.png&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>The author applies Monte Carlo tree search algorithms to analyze Starve the Fox, an asymmetric traditional Norse board game played between a fox and a flock of sheep. While conventional wisdom holds that the game heavily favors the fox, deeper computer evaluations demonstrate that coordinated sheep movement can successfully counter the foxes and create winning positions.</p><p>By training regression models to build a heuristic evaluation function, the analysis quantifies the strategic value of board positions, rank advancements, and captured pieces. The findings show that a living fox is worth approximately two living sheep, and that the sheep side must gain nine total ranks to offset the loss of a single captured piece.</p>

<h2>TU Wien and UC San Diego Researchers Extend X-Ray High-Harmonic Generation Beyond Standard Limit</h2>
<a name="story-25771"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fpub.mdpi-res.com%2Fapplsci%2Fapplsci-09-00378%2Farticle_deploy%2Fhtml%2Fimages%2Fapplsci-09-00378-g001.png%3F1570985357&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>Researchers at TU Wien and the University of California San Diego have generated X-rays with energies exceeding the conventional single-electron limit by utilizing correlated electron interactions in helium. By employing precisely controlled ultraviolet laser pulses, the team successfully caused two electrons to return to an atom simultaneously, combining their energies to emit higher-frequency X-ray photons.</p><p>Published in Nature Photonics, the findings challenge the long-standing single-active-electron model of high-harmonic generation established over three decades ago. This simultaneous release of energy creates a secondary plateau of sustained, higher-energy emissions, offering a novel spectroscopic tool for investigating complex quantum mechanics and multi-electron correlations on attosecond timescales.</p>
