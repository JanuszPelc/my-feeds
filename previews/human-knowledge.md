<h2>Jordan Valley Archaeological Site &#x27;Ubeidiya Dated to at Least 1.9 Million Years Ago</h2>
<a name="story-25554"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Farchaeology.org%2Fwp-content%2Fuploads%2F2026%2F02%2FNews-20260225-Jordan-Valley-Stone-Tool-590x1024.jpg&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>Researchers have redated the archaeological site of 'Ubeidiya in the Jordan Valley to at least 1.9 million years ago, pushing back the timeline of early human migration into the Levant by hundreds of thousands of years. Using a combination of cosmogenic isotope burial dating, paleomagnetism, and uranium-lead dating of fossilized freshwater snail shells, the team established that the site is contemporaneous with early human activity at Dmanisi in Georgia.</p><p>The updated timeline indicates that early human populations expanded out of Africa during a crucial early period, bringing distinct stone tool traditions—including both Oldowan and Acheulean technologies—into new regions. Accounting for a complex history of Pleistocene sediment recycling within the Dead Sea rift resolved initial discrepancies in isotope measurements, strengthening the reliability of the new chronological framework.</p>

<h2>University of Bonn Researchers Discover Two Distinct Sleep Stages in Australian Jumping Spiders</h2>
<a name="story-25553"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fscx1.b-cdn.net%2Fcsz%2Fnews%2F800a%2F2026%2Fhow-deeply-do-spiders-2.jpg&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>Researchers at the University of Bonn have found that the Australian jumping spider Portia fimbriata experiences distinct sleep stages, including a phase closely resembling rapid eye movement (REM) sleep in vertebrates. Using infrared cameras and behavioral response tests under various lighting conditions, the team observed that the spiders exhibit muscle twitches and characteristic eye movements during sleep, while also demonstrating sleep rebound after periods of sleep disruption.</p><p>The findings provide experimental confirmation that these spiders engage in genuine, regulated sleep rather than simple resting behavior. While the presence of REM-like movements does not confirm that spiders dream, the discovery offers new insight into the evolution and complexity of sleep across different nervous systems, opening avenues for future research into how sleep supports cognitive functions and is affected by environmental factors.</p>

<h2>OpenAI AI Disproves Exponential Time Hypothesis</h2>
<a name="story-25527"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fneurohive.io%2Fwp-content%2Fuploads%2F2022%2F02%2FSnimok-ekrana-2022-02-04-v-14.34.15.png&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>The Exponential Time Hypothesis (ETH) has been disproved by OpenAI's AI models through a deterministic algorithm that counts satisfying assignments of fixed-width CNF formulas in subexponential time and polynomial space.</p><p>The discovery was one of three scientific breakthroughs in mathematics, computer science, and physics announced by the company. Scientists who originally proposed the ETH will lead an independent verification of the findings.</p>

<h2>Google DeepMind AI Model Outperforms Traditional Weather Models in Hurricane Isaias Forecasts</h2>
<a name="story-25459"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fnpr.brightspotcdn.com%2Fdims3%2Fdefault%2Fstrip%2Ffalse%2Fcrop%2F2000x1333%2B0%2B0%2Fresize%2F1100%2Fquality%2F50%2Fformat%2Fwebp%2F%3Furl%3Dhttp%253A%252F%252Fnpr-brightspot.s3.amazonaws.com%252Fcf%252F87%252F99e0849e47e2813d852eb94b5f40%252Fap26282622911128.jpg&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>Meteorologists and hurricane experts report that artificial intelligence models, specifically Google DeepMind's system, are rapidly transforming weather forecasting. When Hurricane Isaias made landfall as a Category 2 storm in early October, the Google DeepMind AI model accurately predicted its path toward the western Florida Panhandle while conventional physics-based models incorrectly projected a more westward track toward southeastern Louisiana.</p><p>While traditional models rely on real-time atmospheric data and complex physical equations, AI models analyze thousands of historical storms using neural networks to identify patterns. The National Hurricane Center incorporated Google's model into its operational forecasts beginning last year, though forecasters continue to use a blend of models and human expertise because the AI system was less effective at predicting storm intensity compared to its accuracy in tracking paths.</p>

<h2>Study of 709 BCE Total Solar Eclipse Refines Earth Rotation and Solar Activity Models</h2>
<a name="story-25449"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fcontent.cld.iop.org%2Fjournals%2F2041-8205%2F995%2F1%2FL1%2Frevision1%2Fapjlae0461f1_hr.jpg&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>An international research team has analyzed records of a total solar eclipse from 709 BCE preserved in ancient Chinese historical texts to reconstruct Earth's historical rotation speed. By correcting an eight-kilometer error in the geographic coordinates of the ancient Lu capital of Qufu, the scientists calculated a delta T value between 20,264 and 21,204 seconds, providing more precise constraints on how fast Earth spun during the eighth through sixth centuries BCE.</p><p>Investigators also examined a later historical addendum from the Book of Han describing the eclipsed Sun as "completely yellow above and below," interpreting it as a possible early written account of the solar corona. This observation aligns with radiocarbon-based tree ring reconstructions suggesting the Sun was transitioning out of a prolonged period of low activity known as the Neo Assyrian Grand Minimum.</p>
<p><small>Links: <a href="https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/10/261025073055.htm">sciencedaily.com</a> | <a href="https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.3847/2041-8213/ae0461">source</a></small></p>

<h2>Dianjunus Rex Giant Fungus Discovered in China</h2>
<a name="story-25452"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fs.yimg.com%2Flo%2Fmysterio%2Fapi%2Fd6381035bd180382f44aac6c3189d22c28a47132491df4f1e036bf23c697a46f%2Flightyear_networkapi%2Fresizefill_w1200%3Bquality_80%3Bformat_webp%2Fhttps%3A%2F%2Fmedia.zenfs.com%2Fen%2Fcnn_articles_875%2F545f470adfcbea1c527f581b09434e74.jpg&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>Scientists have discovered a new giant fungal species, Dianjunus rex (nicknamed "D-rex"), in the forests of Yunnan, China. Producing upright reproductive structures reaching up to 50 centimeters in height and weighing up to 2.2 kilograms, it represents the largest known fruiting bodies within the phylum Ascomycota and establishes an entirely new genus and family, Dianjunaceae.</p><p>Genetic analysis and molecular clock estimates indicate that the lineage of Dianjunus rex originated approximately 65 million years ago, roughly one million years after the Cretaceous-Paleogene mass extinction. Researchers suggest that the dramatic ecological disruption and sudden abundance of dead plant material following the extinction event created unique opportunities for wood-decomposing fungi, potentially driving the evolution of its large spore-dispersal structures.</p>

<h2>University of Illinois Researchers Develop First 3D Thermal Invisibility Cloak</h2>
<p>Researchers have developed and tested the first three-dimensional thermal invisibility cloak capable of concealing objects from infrared cameras and extreme temperatures from any direction. Constructed from a hybrid aluminum-and-rubber lattice material, the device directs heat around a hidden object rather than simply blocking it, creating a uniform thermal profile that makes the object appear absent to infrared imaging.</p><p>Published in Nature Communications, the breakthrough overcomes the geometric and directional limitations of earlier two-dimensional thermal cloaks. The technology could improve thermal management in microchips, protect sensitive electronics from harsh environments, and offer new methods for thermal stealth, with future research aiming to adapt the design for objects that generate their own heat.</p>
<p><small>Links: <a href="https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/10/261025073051.htm">sciencedaily.com</a> | <a href="https://news.illinois.edu/new-3d-thermal-cloak-hides-objects-from-heat-in-any-direction/">source</a></small></p>

<h2>Magnetic Skyrmion Lattices Filmed Melting in Real Time to Advance Brain-Like Computing</h2>

<p>Researchers have recorded the real-time melting of a magnetic skyrmion lattice while simultaneously manipulating the shape and size of individual skyrmions. Conducted primarily at Johannes Gutenberg University Mainz alongside contributions from the Norwegian University of Science and Technology, the study offers unprecedented insight into the dynamics of these microscopic magnetic vortices.</p><p>Skyrmions are nanoscale magnetic structures that could enable high-density data storage, quantum technologies, and brain-like neuromorphic computing architectures operating with drastically reduced electricity consumption. By demonstrating precise, real-time control over how these ordered lattices break apart and behave individually, the findings help overcome fundamental obstacles toward developing practical spintronic computing devices.</p>

<h2>Kekova Shipwreck May Be World&#x27;s Oldest</h2>
<a name="story-25453"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fbrusselsmorning.com%2Fwp-content%2Fuploads%2FWorlds-Oldest-Shipwreck-Found-Off-Turkey-Coast-Near-Kekova.webp&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>Archaeologists have tentatively dated a shipwreck off the coast of Kekova, Turkey, to approximately 2700–2400 BCE. Based on ceramic pottery findings, the discovery may be the world's oldest known shipwreck, potentially predating the 4,200-year-old wreck found off the coast of Dokos, Greece.</p><p>The cargo includes amphorae that may have contained olive oil, wine, or tree resin, providing evidence of extensive maritime trade networks during the Early Bronze Age. This find could transform current understanding of commodity exchange across the eastern Mediterranean.</p>

The map utilizes swisstopo vector and raster datasets for domestic terrain and infrastructure, supplemented by Copernicus elevation data for areas beyond national borders.</p>

<h2>UC Berkeley Math Professor Richard Borcherds Stops Grading Homework Due to AI Use</h2>
<a name="story-25408"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fnypost.com%2Fwp-content%2Fuploads%2Fsites%2F2%2F2026%2F10%2Frichard-borcherds-uc-berkeley-professor-146464780.jpg%3Fquality%3D75%26strip%3Dall%26w%3D1288&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>UC Berkeley mathematics professor and Fields medalist Richard Borcherds has stopped grading student homework for correctness after observing that widespread artificial intelligence use allows students to consistently achieve top scores. Borcherds currently evaluates assignments solely on completion, stating that paying graders to verify accuracy has become pointless because AI-generated solutions are unavoidable.</p><p>The decision follows recent advancements in mathematical artificial intelligence capabilities, including OpenAI's announcement regarding the Navier-Stokes existence and smoothness problem. Borcherds expressed uncertainty about the future of mathematics as a career path and noted that while campus departments encourage faculty to establish clear artificial intelligence policies in syllabi, standard detection tools remain inadequate.</p>

<h2>Max Planck Institute Researchers Discover Three Supermassive Black Holes in Distant Galaxy</h2>
<a name="story-25371"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fexpatguideturkey.com%2Fwp-content%2Fuploads%2F2025%2F11%2F23b64fc6-667f-4e86-bedd-37417b4f4e0e.jpeg&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>Astronomers using the James Webb Space Telescope have discovered three actively feeding supermassive black holes in the distant galaxy J0148-4214, located more than 12.5 billion light-years from Earth. Led by the Max Planck Institute for Extraterrestrial Physics, an international team identified the system by analyzing high-speed hydrogen gas movements using integral field spectroscopy.</p><p>Two of the black holes reside near the galaxy's center and are projected to merge within the next few hundred million years, while a third sits further out and may have been displaced by an earlier gravitational recoil. The discovery provides rare evidence of how efficient early universe processes were at bringing massive black holes together, helping explain their rapid growth shortly after the Big Bang.</p>

<h2>Yale University Study Suggests Spinosaurus Spent Most of Life Underwater</h2>
<a name="story-25375"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fs3.animalia.bio%2Fdinosauropedia%2Fdinosaurs%2Fphotos%2Fresize%2F900%2Fspinosaurus-palaeoecological-reconstruction.webp%3Fid%3D9c54003c7598c7676e518a5b2a3ddfab&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>Researchers at Yale University have developed a machine learning approach to reconstruct the lifestyles of extinct vertebrates, concluding that the predatory dinosaur Spinosaurus spent the vast majority of its life submerged underwater. By analyzing skeletal measurements from hundreds of fossil and modern specimens alongside a statistical method originally developed during World War II, the team evaluated limb proportions to predict soft-tissue swimming adaptations and aquatic habits with high accuracy.</p><p>The findings provide new support for a heavily aquatic interpretation of Spinosaurus, contrasting with other ancient marine reptiles like mesosaurs, whose limb proportions indicate they regularly moved between land and water. The researchers found that all Paleozoic marine reptiles retained amphibious habits and regularly returned to land, highlighting distinct evolutionary pathways in secondary aquatic adaptation.</p>

<h2>University of Utah Researchers Identify Mushroom Behind Lilliputian Hallucinations</h2>
<a name="story-25374"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fmedia.assettype.com%2Fmedbound%2F2026-03-16%2Fq7mvuw5m%2FMedBound-Times-Image-Edits-57.png%3Fw%3D1200%26auto%3Dformat%2Ccompress%26fit%3Dmax&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>Researchers at the University of Utah used DNA sequencing to identify the mushroom Lanmaoa asiatica as the cause of Lilliputian hallucinations—vivid, detailed visions of miniature humans interacting with their surroundings—in Southwest China and the northern Philippines. Chemical and genetic testing revealed that the mushroom lacks known psychoactive compounds like psilocybin, pointing to an entirely unfamiliar hallucinogenic substance.</p><p>Genomic analysis of 53 specimens across the broader Lanmaoa genus also uncovered four previously unknown species, demonstrating how field research and museum collections can reveal new biological diversity. Isolating the mushroom's mysterious active compound could provide researchers with a novel tool to investigate the neurological mechanisms that generate complex visual hallucinations in humans.</p>
