<h2>Polish Academy of Sciences Researchers Analyze Artemis I Radiation Data Using Lithium Fluoride Detectors</h2>
<a name="story-25681"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.spacevoyaging.com%2Fwp-content%2Fuploads%2F2024%2F09%2FArtemisImannequins.jpeg&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>Researchers at the Institute of Nuclear Physics of the Polish Academy of Sciences have analyzed data collected by experimental lithium fluoride detectors aboard NASA’s uncrewed Artemis I mission. The compact detectors, placed inside human-shaped mannequins during the 2022 lunar flight, recorded cosmic radiation exposure and particle tracks using fluorescence microscopy without requiring chemical etching.</p><p>The findings indicate that radiation doses near the Moon are distributed more uniformly through the body than in low Earth orbit, where trapped protons from Earth's Van Allen belts create uneven exposure patterns. While total radiation levels near the Moon are higher due to galactic cosmic rays, the researchers observed that the overall increase is smaller than typically assumed.</p>

<h2>Transposable Elements Linked to Rapid Diversification of Ants</h2>
<a name="story-25645"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fmdpi-res.com%2Fijms%2Fijms-23-04755%2Farticle_deploy%2Fhtml%2Fimages%2Fijms-23-04755-g001.png&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>Transposable elements, or "jumping genes," likely fueled the evolutionary diversification of ants following the mass extinction 66 million years ago. Surges in the activity of these mobile DNA segments coincided with the expansion of major ant lineages during the early Paleogene, facilitating the high species richness seen in modern ant families.</p><p>Genomic comparisons of 163 ant species show that lineages with more jumping genes tend to be more species-rich. These genetic elements also appear to have contributed to the expansion of gene families responsible for odorant receptors, enhancing the chemical communication systems essential to ant social behavior.</p>
<p><small>Links: <a href="https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/10/261026225608.htm">sciencedaily.com</a> | <a href="https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.aee0306">source</a></small></p>

<h2>David Harvey Discusses Recently Proposed Sub-O(n log n) Integer Multiplication Algorithm</h2>
<a name="story-25157"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fimage.slideserve.com%2F1427678%2Finteger-multiplication-mit-notes-and-wikipedia1-l.jpg&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>Mathematician David Harvey has published a commentary examining a recently proposed integer multiplication algorithm that claims a complexity of o(n log n) bit operations. The analysis offers perspective on the new complexity claim and its relation to established theoretical limits in computational mathematics.</p><p>The discussion follows earlier milestones in the field, including Harvey and Joris van der Hoeven's 2019 algorithm achieving O(n log n) bit operations, which confirmed a long-standing conjecture by Schönhage and Strassen. The newly proposed approach pushes past that established boundary into sub-O(n log n) territory, prompting careful scrutiny of its methods and assumptions.</p>

<h2>Reflective Atmosphere Discovered on Lava Planet TOI-561 b</h2>
<a name="story-25550"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fspacetoday.com.br%2Fwp-content%2Fuploads%2F2025%2F12%2FJAMESWEBBTOI_01.png&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>Observations using the James Webb Space Telescope indicate that the ultra-hot rocky exoplanet TOI-561 b is surrounded by a substantial atmosphere containing reflective silicate clouds. Researchers monitored the planet over 37 hours and found that it reflects approximately 75 percent of incoming starlight while redistributing heat toward its nightside.</p><p>The findings challenge current theories suggesting that heavily irradiated lava planets should have lost their atmospheres billions of years ago. Scientists are investigating whether the atmosphere is continuously replenished by gases escaping from a global magma ocean, with future observations aimed at determining its exact chemical composition.</p>

<h2>Center for Astrophysics Researchers Unveil Lumina Simulation of Early Universe</h2>
<a name="story-25551"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fscx1.b-cdn.net%2Fcsz%2Fnews%2F800a%2F2026%2Fsmithsonian-scientists-2.jpg&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>Researchers at the Center for Astrophysics | Harvard &amp; Smithsonian have developed Lumina, a detailed cosmological simulation that reconstructs the first two billion years after the Big Bang. Published in Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society, the model simulates interactions between ordinary matter, dark matter, and radiation across a volume of space more than 100 times larger than comparable previous projects.</p><p>Powered by more than 15,000 graphics processing units on the Frontier supercomputer at Oak Ridge National Laboratory, the simulation tracks the evolution of the intergalactic medium, galaxies, and black holes while simultaneously modeling hydrogen and helium reionization. Scientists plan to use Lumina to interpret observations from instruments such as the James Webb Space Telescope and evaluate how well existing theoretical models describe cosmic evolution.</p>

<h2>UC San Diego Engineers Develop Light-Based Magnetism Control for Faster Memory</h2>
<a name="story-25552"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fscx2.b-cdn.net%2Fgfx%2Fnews%2F2022%2Fa-novel-all-optical-sw.jpg&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>Engineers at the University of California San Diego have demonstrated a method for controlling magnetism using shaped laser light, overcoming previous thickness and polarization barriers in optical switching. By shrinking and reshaping ultrafast laser beams, the researchers successfully induced deterministic magnetic switching in a nine-layer cobalt and platinum structure without needing specific light polarization.</p><p>While traditional magnetic memory relies on external fields that limit writing speed and consume high energy, optical switching could theoretically achieve speeds over 1,000 times greater. However, significant engineering challenges remain before the specialized ultrafast lasers used in the experiments can be integrated into practical computer hardware.</p>

<h2>Jordan Valley Archaeological Site &#x27;Ubeidiya Dated to at Least 1.9 Million Years Ago</h2>
<a name="story-25554"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Farchaeology.org%2Fwp-content%2Fuploads%2F2026%2F02%2FNews-20260225-Jordan-Valley-Stone-Tool-590x1024.jpg&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>Researchers have redated the archaeological site of 'Ubeidiya in the Jordan Valley to at least 1.9 million years ago, pushing back the timeline of early human migration into the Levant by hundreds of thousands of years. Using a combination of cosmogenic isotope burial dating, paleomagnetism, and uranium-lead dating of fossilized freshwater snail shells, the team established that the site is contemporaneous with early human activity at Dmanisi in Georgia.</p><p>The updated timeline indicates that early human populations expanded out of Africa during a crucial early period, bringing distinct stone tool traditions—including both Oldowan and Acheulean technologies—into new regions. Accounting for a complex history of Pleistocene sediment recycling within the Dead Sea rift resolved initial discrepancies in isotope measurements, strengthening the reliability of the new chronological framework.</p>

<h2>University of Bonn Researchers Discover Two Distinct Sleep Stages in Australian Jumping Spiders</h2>
<a name="story-25553"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fscx1.b-cdn.net%2Fcsz%2Fnews%2F800a%2F2026%2Fhow-deeply-do-spiders-2.jpg&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>Researchers at the University of Bonn have found that the Australian jumping spider Portia fimbriata experiences distinct sleep stages, including a phase closely resembling rapid eye movement (REM) sleep in vertebrates. Using infrared cameras and behavioral response tests under various lighting conditions, the team observed that the spiders exhibit muscle twitches and characteristic eye movements during sleep, while also demonstrating sleep rebound after periods of sleep disruption.</p><p>The findings provide experimental confirmation that these spiders engage in genuine, regulated sleep rather than simple resting behavior. While the presence of REM-like movements does not confirm that spiders dream, the discovery offers new insight into the evolution and complexity of sleep across different nervous systems, opening avenues for future research into how sleep supports cognitive functions and is affected by environmental factors.</p>

<h2>OpenAI AI Disproves Exponential Time Hypothesis</h2>

<h2>Google DeepMind AI Model Outperforms Traditional Weather Models in Hurricane Isaias Forecasts</h2>
<a name="story-25459"><img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fnpr.brightspotcdn.com%2Fdims3%2Fdefault%2Fstrip%2Ffalse%2Fcrop%2F2000x1333%2B0%2B0%2Fresize%2F1100%2Fquality%2F50%2Fformat%2Fwebp%2F%3Furl%3Dhttp%253A%252F%252Fnpr-brightspot.s3.amazonaws.com%252Fcf%252F87%252F99e0849e47e2813d852eb94b5f40%252Fap26282622911128.jpg&amp;w=1200&amp;h=1200&amp;fit=inside&amp;we&amp;output=jpg&amp;bg=white" width="30%" align="right" /></a>
<p>Meteorologists and hurricane experts report that artificial intelligence models, specifically Google DeepMind's system, are rapidly transforming weather forecasting. When Hurricane Isaias made landfall as a Category 2 storm in early October, the Google DeepMind AI model accurately predicted its path toward the western Florida Panhandle while conventional physics-based models incorrectly projected a more westward track toward southeastern Louisiana.</p><p>While traditional models rely on real-time atmospheric data and complex physical equations, AI models analyze thousands of historical storms using neural networks to identify patterns. The National Hurricane Center incorporated Google's model into its operational forecasts beginning last year, though forecasters continue to use a blend of models and human expertise because the AI system was less effective at predicting storm intensity compared to its accuracy in tracking paths.</p>
